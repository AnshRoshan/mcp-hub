# Competitive Research

Use this skill whenever asked to research competitors, alternatives, or the landscape around a product or decision.

## Method

1. **Define the comparison axes first.** What decisions will this research feed (pricing, positioning, features, adoption)? Pick the 5-8 dimensions that matter for those decisions — do not compare everything.
2. **Build the candidate list.** The obvious competitors, then adjacent tools users switch between, then the "no-tool" option (doing it manually). Include open-source and self-hosted options where relevant.
3. **Go to primary sources.** Official docs, pricing pages, changelogs, repos (stars, activity, license), and the product itself if you can run it. Treat marketing copy and third-party reviews as secondary.
4. **Verify anything load-bearing** on two independent sources: pricing tiers, limits, and claims like "supports X" change fast.
5. **Date every observation.** "As of <month year>" on each row; a landscape snapshot without dates misleads.

## Output

- A comparison table on the defined axes, with sources cited per cell.
- **Positioning insight:** where the alternatives cluster, what the gaps are, and what would be genuinely hard to copy.
- Honest limits: what you could not verify, and which claims come from the vendor's own marketing.
